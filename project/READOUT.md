# Readout

The document your prototype ends with. Written for the people who own the
problem, not for the people who built the thing. Every number in it comes from
your workspace, and a number you cannot point to does not go in.

## The headline

Three sentences. Who has the problem. What you built. The one number that
says whether it works. No architecture.

## One worked example

One real question from your eval cases, the answer the prototype gave, and
what a person would have done instead. Not a box-and-arrow diagram.

## The numbers

| What was measured | Result | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| | | |

Add the row that says **what it does not cover**. That row is the one readers
trust you for.

## Cost against the human it replaces

Tokens per request times price, per day, next to what the same work costs
today. Say which one is the lookup and which one is the measurement.

## What it takes to run for real

Where it runs, who is on call, what breaks first, and a realistic timeline.
The deployment checklist in your workspace is the source.

## Risks

The risk register rows that still have no evidence, and the attack that
walked through.

## Recommendation

One of three, and only one: **proceed**, **narrow**, or **stop**. Stop is a
legitimate answer and is scored as one.

## Handover

Where the code is, where the workspace is, how to rerun the evals, and who
told you what during the week.
